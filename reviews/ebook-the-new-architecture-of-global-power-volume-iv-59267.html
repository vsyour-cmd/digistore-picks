Opening copy

Raw materials must reach processing centers. Components must move between factories. Energy must travel from producers to consumers. Finished goods must cross oceans, ports, borders, railways, highways, terminals, and distribution networks before reaching markets.
But connection also creates dependency. When essential flows become concentrated through particular ports, maritime chokepoints, pipelines, transport corridors, logistics hubs, or transit states , physical networks can acquire geopolitical significance.
The Architecture of Connectivity Power examines when these connections become sources of strategic leverage—and why infrastructure, geography, ownership, or high traffic volumes alone do not automatically create power.
